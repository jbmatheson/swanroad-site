Old English poets did not always call the sea the sea. They had a habit of naming a thing by what it was to something else: the sea was the *whale-road*, or the *swan-road*. The figure is called a kenning. In *Beowulf*, the hero sails to Denmark “ofer swanrāde”, over the swan-road.

The name is that kenning. The game is a stretch of water between two shores, with pearls on it that drift toward whoever pulls hardest. A road is a path things travel along. A swan’s road is the sea seen as that path.

## What the pieces are

The names of the pieces follow from there. The pulling pieces are anchors, because anchors are the thing on a ship that holds against the water. The pieces that drift are pearls, because they begin in an oyster bed on the middle row, which is why the starting squares are marked with shells. The two sides are Light and Dark: a sand-coloured anchor and an iron one.

The tide is the tide. It comes in on a schedule, it takes what is lying on the beach, and it comes early if nothing on the water has moved for a while.

## The road in the daily game

The daily game is the part of the app where the name earns its keep. One board a day, the same for everyone, one try. Everybody walks the same road that day and compares how far they got. The leaderboard counts turns to win, and resets at midnight.

## Saying it

It is *Swan Road*: two words, no hyphen and no apostrophe. The kenning, said plainly. The app icon is a pearl above the water with its light running down to an anchor on the sand, which is the whole game in one picture, and the splash screen is the same picture painted larger.
